# Common skincare actives explained
Retinoids (retinol, retinal, adapalene, tretinoin) speed up cell turnover and are among the best-studied ingredients for acne, texture and fine lines. Start two or three nights a week, use a pea-sized amount, and expect some dryness at first. Always wear sunscreen. Retinoids are generally avoided during pregnancy, so ask a doctor.
Niacinamide (vitamin B3) helps with oil control, redness, uneven tone and the skin barrier. It is well tolerated and works at 2 to 5 percent for most people.
AHAs such as glycolic and lactic acid exfoliate the surface and help with dullness and texture. BHA (salicylic acid) is oil-soluble, gets into pores and is a favorite for blackheads and oily, acne-prone skin.
Vitamin C (ascorbic acid and derivatives) is an antioxidant used in the morning under sunscreen to help with dullness and dark spots.
Azelaic acid helps acne, redness and hyperpigmentation and is usually gentle enough for sensitive skin.
Avoid using strong retinoids and strong acids on the same night when you start; alternate them to limit irritation.
